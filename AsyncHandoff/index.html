02Motivation

A complete action should not stop the stream.

A non-streaming model prepares an expressive, complete action. An interactive character still needs something to play while that action is being generated—and a way to keep moving afterwards.

How do we reuse the full action without giving up continuous execution?

Streaming T2M

Generates successive segments and responds during playback; the action itself is synthesized by the streaming model.

Non-streaming T2M

Prepares a complete clip before playback; entering it from the current motion and continuing afterwards remain open.

AsyncHandoff

Responds with streaming motion, connects to the complete external action, preserves it, then resumes streaming from its final history.

03Method

Prepare ahead. Hand off at the anchor.

Both generators stay frozen. A small connector refines an analytic bridge from the reserved motion history into the target; the target itself bypasses the network, and its last frames condition the streaming model.

01

Respond

The streaming model keeps supplying motion while the complete target is being generated.

02

Connect

At a reserved future boundary, the connector adds bounded corrections to an analytic bridge; candidates are screened before commit.

03

Preserve

The target follows on its own path: every native frame, in order, with no blending or regeneration.

04

Continue

The target's last 13 frames condition prefetched streaming continuation—no exit connector.

The ordered bridge search is 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 frames. Each candidate must pass kinematic admission checks (seam steps, acceleration, jerk, bone length, foot speed and floor height) before it is queued; otherwise the streaming motion simply continues and the rejection is recorded.

05Rendering

De-jittering and IK, after generation.

Every video on this page is rendered from a postprocessed reconstruction of the displayed motion. The same output stage ships with the code and is on by default; it never feeds back into the model.

inputDisplayed motion22 joints, 20 Hz, original timestamps
01De-jitterSavitzky–Golay filter, window 7, order 2
02RotationsTwist-resolved local frames; bounded SMPL-H limb fit (≤ 0.12 rad)
03SO(3) smoothing7-tap relative-rotation filter, two passes
04AnatomyNeck/ankle cones; hinge knees and elbows; neutral head, wrists, fingers
05Leg IK + floorTwo-bone IK to source ankles, then residual floor clearance; fresh skinning

What it changes

Only the rendered reconstruction: joint jitter, rotational noise, implausible joint limits and floor penetration in the display.

What it keeps

Native 263-D features, every target frame and its order, frame ownership and player timestamps. Mean display deviation from source joints is 3.8–4.3 cm per provider.

What it is not

Not part of any reported metric, not a zero-lookahead online filter, and not a foot-locking or physics solver.
